## What the product does
Copy-trading. A client's MetaMask Hybrid smart account (owner() = client's wallet) gives our executor key TWO ERC-7710 delegations. (1) Gate delegation: target = this gate only; methods buy, sell, payFee, refundGas, withdraw; expiry 90 days; limitedCalls 100000; **no value limit of any kind in the delegation** (no valueLte, no period cap). (2) Approve delegation (as today): any token, method approve only, spender pinned to the gate by an allowedCalldata caveat on word 0, valueLte 0, expiry. NO CUSTODY: bought tokens stay in the client's account; a sale pulls them with transferFrom through the approve. When the executor redeems, `msg.sender` inside the gate is the client's account. All value limits live in the gate.

## What changed against v17.2
1. **Fee** `payFee()` payable -> `FEE_TO` (immutable) only. Let `before = msg.sender.balance + msg.value` (balance before the transfer). Require `value <= 20% of before` and, per account in a sliding 30-day window (a ring of sub-buckets since round 3, every slot of the last 30 to 35 days counted in full), `feeSum + value <= 20% of (before + feeSum + wdSum)`, where `wdSum` is native sent by `withdrawTo` to an address that is neither the account nor its owner. `withdraw()` credits nothing. Rationale: no compounding of repeated 20% calls; honest 15%-of-profit charging with withdrawals in between always fits. View `feeRoom(account)`.
2. **Gas** `refundGas()` payable -> `FEE_TO`: `GAS_CALL_MAX` per call, `GAS_DAY_MAX` per account per sliding day, and the day total also <= 5% of the balance. The hourly buy cap is a ring window too (17.5 HYPE in any 60 minutes).
3. **Withdraw** `withdraw()` payable -> `ownerOf(msg.sender)`, any amount, never paused. `ownerOf`: no code or EIP-7702 designator -> the account itself; otherwise bounded `staticcall owner()` (100k gas, 32 bytes), non-zero, != account/gate/router, itself a plain wallet. `withdrawTo(address)` payable: account-only fallback with a caller-chosen recipient (not 0/gate/router), NOT in the delegation's AllowedMethods.
4. **Sells** have no count or proceeds cap. Buys keep `MAX_BUY_ETH_PER_HOUR` per account (planned 17.5 HYPE). Executor `sell()` (delegated; blocked when paused) keeps the loss budget [rounds 1-3 added a **price reference**, removed in round 4; the text that follows describes it as history] `ref[account][token]` (per account since round 2; high-water of the implied price of that account's own executor buys/sells >= 0.001 HYPE, linear fade over 2 days, +50% max per trade of the faded value): a sale below 70% of it books the shortfall as loss (`_settle`, `_refPrice`, `_refNote`). `exitSell()` (NOT delegated): requires `tx.origin == ownerOf(msg.sender)`, ignores budget and reference, books no loss, works while paused. `exit()` (cold key via tx.origin) as before. `setExitMode` is gone. New lots need >= 0.001 HYPE spend (`DustLot`); `forgetLot(token)` (account-only) deletes a lot without reading the token; `_trimAll` stops after 600k gas with a resume cursor.
5. **Pause** also blocks payFee/refundGas (and still buy/sell); never withdraw, withdrawTo, forgetLot, exit, exitSell.
6. **Token calls** (`transfer`, `transferFrom`, `approve`) are assembly calls copying <= 32 bytes; no return data = success, else one word == 1. The gate's own balance reads use the bounded `_balanceOf` (100k gas, floor 102k).
7. **Constructor** takes a struct `{router, executor, guardian, exitKey, feeTo, maxBuyEthPerHour, gasCallMax, gasDayMax}`.
8. **Unchanged** v17.2 logic: lot book with reconciliation (`_trim`, `_trimAll`, `reconcile`, 32-lot registry), `_checkRoute`, loss budget + `unlock`, hourly buy window, guardian pause/rotation, `replaceGuardian`, `exit`, `lock` guard, ETH accepted only mid-call.

## Invariants we believe hold
- I1 The gate holds no HYPE and no ERC-20 between calls (stray transfers excluded).
- I2 Native leaves a client's account through the gate only to: ROUTER (buy, <= hourly cap; unspent change and kickbacks return to the account), `FEE_TO` (fee <= 20% rule, gas caps), `ownerOf(account)` (withdraw), or, when the account itself calls, the recipient it names (`withdrawTo`). Sale proceeds go to the account.
- I3 Fees in a 30-day window never exceed 20% of (opening balance + deposits + sale proceeds) of that account (fuzzed).
- I4 `costOf[a] == basis[a][*].ethIn` summed over the registry; a lot never exceeds what the account holds after a reconcile.
- I5 No caller can debit another account's lot, fee window, or loss record.
- I6 A stolen executor key acting through the two delegations cannot send value to an address of its choosing, cannot raise a cap, cannot reach withdrawTo/forgetLot/exitSell/unlock/exit; per account it is bounded by the hourly buy cap, the 30%/7-day loss budget (below cost), the 20% fee rule and the gas caps; it can withdraw any amount but only to owner() (denial of service, not theft).

## Questions for the reviewer
1. Fee rule: is `before = msg.sender.balance + msg.value` correct for every account type (Hybrid, 7702 EOA, a smart account whose validation or earlier batch calls move value in the same transaction)? Any way to inflate `before` or `wdSum` (self-owned account, owner() that forwards back, withdraw sequences, sale round trips, deposits by the attacker's own money) to extract more than 20% of the true account value per window? Is the ring window (span to span + 1/6 span) sound, and does it have rounding or bucket-skipping gaps?
2. Removing every value limit from the delegation: what is the worst a stolen executor key does through buy/sell/payFee/refundGas/withdraw per hour/day? In particular the hourly buy window (ring hour, `buyEth` counts spent net of refunds and `home` kickbacks), and the removal of a daily ceiling.
3. Sells without count/proceeds caps: with the loss budget cost-based, a stolen key can sell profitable lots through a rigged route (srcReceivers chosen by the router data) and divert the surplus (L4). [Round 4: the price reference was removed; the profit leak is a documented residual watched off-chain.] Rounds 1-3 text: we added the price reference as a cheap bound; please attack it (poisoning upwards, waiting out the fade, tokens with no history, the 1.5x step, rounding, partial sells, interaction with the budget) and say whether a better cheap bound exists. Is `exitSell`'s `tx.origin == ownerOf(msg.sender)` proof sound (phishing contracts reaching the gate as the account in the owner's transaction; 7702 accounts; the Hybrid account with a self-delegation to the owner EOA; a bundler/UserOp not qualifying)?
4. `withdraw`/`ownerOf`: anything that makes a withdrawal pay a non-owner; `owner()` staticcall bounding; 7702 designator check; reverting owner and the `withdrawTo` fallback.
5. Reconciliation/lot book as in v17.2 with the new paths: do payFee/refundGas/withdraw change `account.balance` in a way that lets the executor shrink or inflate the loss-budget base (`costOf + balance`) to its advantage?
6. Token calls: the new assembly `_call3` (selector as bytes4, 68/100-byte calldata, 32-byte return); `_pull` measuring the gate's balance delta; `_gateBal` replacing the unbounded IERC20 reads.
7. Native/Orbit specifics: `gas()`/GAS_FLOOR under ArbOS, `tx.origin` for `exit` and `exitSell`, `block.timestamp` with sub-second blocks for the hour/day/30-day windows and the 7-day refill, opcodes (PUSH0 and MCOPY only beyond Shanghai, as the live bytecode).
8. Anything the single gate delegation (no value cap) fails to enforce on its own.

## Known residuals (by design)
Sale `srcReceivers` chosen by the aggregator; buys have no price check beyond the hourly cap and there is no daily buy ceiling; the sell price reference is weak (only recent executor trades, 2-day fade, free down to 70% of it, then bounded by the 30% budget; the executor key can ratchet it up 1.5x per real trade, causing only refused/booked executor sells); with the registry full of junk the executor's new entries fail until the owner calls `forgetLot`; after a big withdrawal fees may be payable only in parts; the sliding windows count `max(cur + prev*w, prev)` (round 2), so a span never carries more than the limit but a full-cap spend blocks further spend for up to two slots; `exitSell` needs the owner's EOA as tx.origin; the guardian can pause but never move funds; a thief can withdraw to the owner (DoS).
